Lesson 2: Rhythm and Groove

Lesson 2 of 25, in Introduction and Drums.

Learning intention

We are learning how groove shapes style in popular music.

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

ListenThe three grooves

LearnSay it out loud.

How does it feel? Which elements make it feel like that? Answer as a class before you move on. The feel chart on the wall has words if you need them.

Table

GrooveWhere the kick and snare landTypical genre
BackbeatKick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4Rock, pub rock
Four-on-the-floorKick on every beat: 1, 2, 3, 4EDM, dance, disco
TrapSparse kick, snare or clap on 3, fast rolling hi-hatsHip hop

Your taskGroove Comparison

Clap each groove as a class. Then on your worksheet, draw a simple icon for each groove, note the tempo, and describe the feel in your own words.

Key words

Beat
the steady pulse of the music. It is what you tap your foot to.
Groove
the overall rhythmic feel created by the interaction of drums, bass and other instruments. Two songs can share a beat but have completely different grooves.

Need a hand?Clap it before you write it

Count “1 - 2 - 3 - 4” out loud and clap only on 2 and 4. That is a backbeat. Now clap on every number. That is four-on-the-floor. You have just played the difference between rock and dance music. For “describe the feel”, pick one: driving, bouncy, relaxed, heavy, floaty, urgent.

Go further

Transcribe one groove into simple notation on a four-beat grid, showing kick, snare and hi-hat on separate lines. Then compare the same groove across three Australian songs - does the tempo or the hi-hat pattern change the feel more?

Exit ticket

Which groove is most iconic in Australian music, and why?
