Liner notes

ICIP protects what a Community owns, even when the words are freely available to hear.

Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property, ICIP, covers a Community's Languages, stories, Songlines, images and Knowledges. It is not the same as copyright, which protects a recording once it exists. ICIP protects the right to say whether that recording should exist, or exist in that form, at all.

Consent is central. A Community, or the person who holds a story, can give permission, place limits on it, or refuse it, and that decision can be withdrawn at any time, even after it has been given. Language is used only where its meaning is known and a local Cultural Knowledge Holder has given permission for it to be used that way.

Ethical use, and what it is not

Ethical useNot ethical use
Language used with permission, its meaning known and understoodLanguage sampled for its sound without knowing what it means
A story told by the person who holds it, or with their guidanceA story retold by someone outside the Community, without permission
The artist and Community credited by nameMusic used with no credit or acknowledgement at all

Your own track, the same rule

The cover and GarageBand track you have built this term sit under the same protocols. This unit does not ask you to compose using Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander Language, Songlines or Dreaming stories, because that needs a Community's collaboration and permission arranged in advance, not something built alone in a classroom.

Key word: ICIP

Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property: a Community's right to control its own Languages, stories, Songlines, images and Knowledges, including whether they are used at all.
