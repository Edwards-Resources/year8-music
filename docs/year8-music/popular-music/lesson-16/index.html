Texture and Layering

Lesson 16 of 25 · Texture, FX and Assessment

We are learning to use texture to build density in music.

Thin and thick

Thin - Vance Joy, Riptide

Thick - Baker Boy, Marryuna (feat. Yirrmal)

Contrast is the point.

A track where everything plays all the time is exhausting. Take layers away in the verse and add them in the chorus, and the chorus feels bigger without being louder.

Add a layer

  1. Choose one new layer: a pad, a countermelody, or extra percussion.
  2. Record it over your existing four layers.
  3. Now mute one layer for the first 8 bars and unmute it after. Listen to the difference.
  4. Decide which layers belong in your quiet section and which in your loud one.
  5. Save.

Key word: Tone colour

Also called timbre. The unique sound quality of an instrument, voice or electronic effect. It is what makes a piano and a guitar sound different playing the same note.

Key word: Dynamics

How loud or soft the music is, and how that changes across a song. Adding layers can make a section feel bigger without turning anything up.

Need a hand? One layer is enough

Add a single pad sound holding one long chord underneath everything. That is a real texture layer and it will make your track sound fuller straight away. Thin texture means few layers, like one voice and a guitar. Thick means many, like a full band with backing vocals.

Go further

Try polyphonic layering - two independent melodies running at once that both work on their own and together. Harder than it sounds, and it is what separates a stack of loops from an arrangement.

Encore

How many layers does your track have now, and which one would you remove first?
