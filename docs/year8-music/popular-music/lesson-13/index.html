Hooks and Riffs

Lesson 13 of 25 · Bass and Hooks

We are learning what makes a hook memorable.

Five kinds of hook

Guitar riff - AC/DC, T.N.T.

Vocal riff - The Easybeats, Friday On My Mind

String hook - The Veronicas, Untouched

Flute riff - Men At Work, Down Under

Xylophone hook - Gotye, Somebody That I Used To Know

What makes a hook stick?

Usually it is short (two bars or less), repeated often, and simple enough to sing back after hearing it once. Complicated ideas are rarely memorable. Notice that these five are played on five different things - guitar, voice, strings, flute and xylophone. A hook is an idea, not an instrument.

Your task - Design your riff

Before you touch GarageBand, plan it. Use three or four notes only. Sing or hum it first - if you cannot sing it, it is too complicated.

Key word: Riff

A repeated melodic or rhythmic pattern, often played on guitar, bass or synth. A riff usually sits underneath or between the vocals.

Key word: Hook

A short, catchy musical idea that makes a song memorable. A riff can be the hook, but a hook can also be a vocal line, a string part or even a rhythm.

Need a hand? Use the three-note model

Start from the teacher's three-note riff and change one thing - a note, the rhythm, or the instrument sound. That is still your riff, and it will work over your chords. Safe notes over a C-G-Am-F progression: C, E, G, A. Any of them will sound right.

Go further

Take your riff and flip it into another genre - play a rock guitar riff as an EDM synth line, or a synth hook on distorted guitar. Same notes, different world. What has to change besides the sound?

Encore

Sing your riff to the person next to you. Can they sing it back?
