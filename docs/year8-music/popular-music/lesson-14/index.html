Recording Your Riff

Lesson 14 of 25 · Bass and Hooks

We are learning to record a riff that gives identity to our composition.

Video

Recording a riff on the virtual guitars

Liner notes

A riff has to survive fifty repeats. That is a very different job from playing something once.

This is the fourth and last of the required layers, and it is the one a listener hears most often. At a typical pop tempo of 120 beats per minute, a two-bar riff running through a three-minute track repeats about forty-five times. Anything slightly annoying on the first repeat is unbearable by the fortieth. That is why the plan in Lesson 13 asked for three or four notes. The fewer moving parts a riff has, the longer it survives.

Quantise means snapping the notes you played onto the nearest line of the beat grid. It does not change which notes you played or how hard you hit them, only when each one starts, so a take with the right feel and loose timing can be rescued instead of rerecorded. Set the quantise value to match the fastest note in your riff. If your riff moves in eighth notes, quantise to 1/8. Quantise that same riff to 1/4 and half your notes get dragged onto beats they were never meant to land on.

Once one take is right, stop playing it. Tap the region, choose Loop, and GarageBand repeats it for you for as far as you drag it. Copying a take that is right is not cheating. It is how nearly every record you listen to is made, and one loop that is exactly in time beats eight live takes that are nearly in time.

If you can hear your riff on its own but it vanishes once all four layers are playing, the problem is almost never volume. It is register, meaning how high or low the part sits. Your bass is at the bottom, your chords fill the middle, and a riff written in the middle of that is fighting the chords for the same space. Move the riff up an octave and it will appear without you touching a single fader. Turn it up only if moving it did not work.

Repetition is what makes a riff memorable and it is also what makes a track boring, which sounds like a contradiction and is not. The professional fix is variation, not novelty. Keep the shape and change one thing: shift the rhythm, alter one note, or drop the riff out altogether for four bars. Bringing something back only works if you took it away.

Record your riff

  1. Add a track and choose the sound you planned in Lesson 13.
  2. Record your riff over the top of drums, chords and bass.
  3. Quantise if the timing is loose.
  4. Copy the region so the riff repeats across the loop.
  5. Listen to all four layers together. Can you still hear the riff, or is it buried? Adjust the volume.
  6. Save.

Key word: Quantise

Snapping recorded notes onto the nearest line of the beat grid so that loose timing becomes exact. It changes when a note starts, not which note it is or how loud it is. Quantise to the value of the fastest note in the part.

In your book

  1. Quantise: copy the definition from the key word box, in a full sentence.
  2. Work out how many times your own riff will repeat. Take the length of your track in bars and divide it by the length of your riff in bars. Write the number down.
  3. Copy the rule for a buried riff: if you cannot hear it, change its register before you change its volume.
  4. Name the one thing you will vary in the second half of your track, and say what that change will do to the listener.

You now have all four required layers

drums, chords, bass and riff. Lessons 16-18 are about shaping them into a finished track.

Need a hand? Record it one note at a time

You do not have to play it live. Draw the notes onto the grid one at a time, in the piano roll. The result sounds identical and you control exactly where each note lands.

Go further

Write a variation of your riff for the second half of the track - same shape, one or two notes changed, or the rhythm displaced. Then try dropping the riff out entirely for four bars before it returns, and listen to what its absence does.

Encore

Which of your four layers is the most interesting to listen to, and why?
