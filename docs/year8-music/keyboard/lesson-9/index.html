Melody and Accompaniment

Lesson 9 of 25 · Rhythm and the Left Hand

We are learning to perform melody and accompaniment together.

The hardest thing so far.

Two hands, doing different jobs, at the same time. Everyone finds this hard and everyone gets there. The trick is not practising harder, it is practising smaller.

Chunking: how to actually get this

  1. Play the left hand alone until you no longer have to think about it.
  2. Play the right hand alone until the same is true.
  3. Play the first bar only, both hands, ridiculously slowly.
  4. Add the second bar. Then the third. Never add a bar until the one before it is easy.

Your task

Hot Cross Buns, right hand melody, left hand drone on C. Use the chunking steps in order. Aim to get two bars solid rather than four bars messy.

Key word: Texture

How many layers of sound are happening at once and how they fit together. One hand on its own is a thin texture. Two hands doing different jobs is a thicker one.

Need a hand? Split the job

Play the melody while your partner plays the drone, then swap. Hearing the two parts together while you are only responsible for one of them makes the coordination much easier when you take both on.

Go further

Change the rhythm of your drone so it is not just on every beat. Keep the melody exactly as it is, and listen to how different the whole piece becomes.
