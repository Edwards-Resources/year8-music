The Left Hand Joins In

Lesson 8 of 25 · Rhythm and the Left Hand

We are learning to perform left hand accompaniments.

Music built over a drone

Bagpipes, where the drones never stop

William Barton and the Sydney Symphony Orchestra

One note, held, changes everything.

A drone is a single note that keeps sounding while the melody moves above it. Bagpipes have pipes that do nothing else. The didgeridoo works the same way. It is the simplest accompaniment there is, and it is the one your left hand learns first.

The bass clef.

Your left hand plays lower notes, so they are written on their own staff with a different symbol at the front. Same idea as the treble clef, different set of note names to learn.

Your task

Left hand only. Put finger 5 on the C below middle C and play it on every beat while your teacher counts. Then play it on beats one and three only. Then two quavers on each beat. Same note the whole time, three different accompaniments.

Key words

DroneAccompanimentBass clef

Drone - a single note held or repeated underneath a melody. Accompaniment - the supporting part that sits under the tune. Bass clef - the symbol for the lower staff, where the left hand usually reads.

Need a hand? One finger, one key

You need one finger on one key. Everything else here is timing. If keeping the beat is the hard part, play along with your teacher counting out loud until it locks in.

Go further

Instead of one note, alternate between C and G in your left hand. Work out why those two sound settled together, and what changes if you use C and D instead.

Encore

Name one instrument that has a drone built into it.
