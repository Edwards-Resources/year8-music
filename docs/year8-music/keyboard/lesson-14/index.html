Hands Together

Lesson 14 of 25 · Scales and Hands Together

We are learning to perform a C major scale hands together.

Video

Hands together. The video runs two octaves. You only need one, so stop at the top of the first.

The two thumbs never land together.

That is what makes this hard. The right thumb tucks at F while the left hand is still descending, and the left hand crosses at A while the right is doing nothing unusual. If you try to feel both, you lose both. Listen instead: if the scale sounds even, it is even.

Four notes at a time

  1. Play C D E F hands together. Stop. Check.
  2. Play G A B C hands together. Stop. Check.
  3. Join the two halves. Slowly.
  4. Only once it is even, add the way back down.

Your task

One octave, hands together, up and down. Four notes at a time. If the hands come apart, go back to the four note chunk where it happened rather than starting again from the top.

Need a hand? Hands separately is a valid result

Hands together is the extension, not the requirement. The skills test accepts the scale hands separately. Get each hand genuinely solid, then try them together with whatever time is left.

Go further

Play the scale hands together at three different tempos, and add a crescendo going up and a diminuendo coming back down.

Encore

At which note does the right hand thumb tuck under?
