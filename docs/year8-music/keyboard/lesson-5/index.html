Step, Leap, Repeat

Lesson 5 of 25 · Pitch and the Keyboard

We are learning to identify pitch movement in music.

Three famous openings

Repeat, then a leap down - Beethoven, Symphony No. 5

Step - John Williams, Jaws main title

Leap - Over the Rainbow, from The Wizard of Oz

Three notes can be enough.

Beethoven repeats one note three times and then drops. Williams uses two notes a semitone apart and nothing else. Over the Rainbow opens by leaping a full octave on the first word. None of these needed to be complicated to become unforgettable.

Your task

Listen to each opening twice and fill in the table. Then draw the shape of each melody as a line going up, down or staying flat. That line is the contour.

Key word: Contour

The shape a melody makes as it rises, falls or stays level. Draw it as a line and you have drawn the contour.

Need a hand? Draw before you name

Do not try to name the movement first. Draw the shape with your finger in the air while it plays, then look at what your finger did. Small moves are steps, big jumps are leaps, no movement is a repeat.

Go further

Notate the first two bars of one of these openings by ear. Get the contour right first, then work on the exact notes.
