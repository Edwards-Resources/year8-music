Writing Rhythm Down

Lesson 7 of 25 · Rhythm and the Left Hand

We are learning to write and read rhythm notation.

Dictation is not a trick.

Your teacher claps, you write it down. It feels impossible the first time and it stops feeling that way quickly, because the skill is really just counting. Four beats in a bar, and each beat holds one sound, two sounds or silence.

How to take a rhythm down

  1. Listen once and write nothing. Just find the beat.
  2. Listen again, tapping the beat with your foot while the rhythm plays.
  3. On the third listen, mark which beats have one sound, two sounds or no sound.
  4. Write the note values in last. The counting is the hard part, not the drawing.

Your task

Four dictations, four bars each. Use the four step method every time, then check against a partner before your teacher shows the answer. Where you disagree, clap both versions and decide which one matches.

Key words

Time signatureBar line

Time signature - the two numbers at the start that tell you how many beats are in each bar. Bar line - the vertical line that divides the music into equal groups of beats.

Need a hand? Long and short first

Forget note names to begin with. Draw a long dash for a long sound and a short dash for a short one, in the order you hear them. Turn the dashes into proper notes afterwards.

Go further

Take down a rhythm that includes semiquavers or a dotted crotchet, and write the counting underneath it.

Encore
