Write a Four Bar Rhythm

Lesson 15 of 25 · Scales and Hands Together

We are learning to compose and perform short rhythmic patterns.

Your turn to write.

You have been reading other people's rhythms all term. Now you write one. Four bars of 4/4, using crotchets, quavers and rests only. It has to be playable, and you have to be the one who plays it.

Rules of the task

  1. Four bars, four beats in each. Count them.
  2. Crotchets, quavers and rests only.
  3. At least one rest somewhere, because silence is part of rhythm.
  4. Repeat something. A pattern that comes back is what makes it sound composed rather than random.

Your task

Write your four bars, count the beats in each one, then perform it. Clap it first, then play it on a single note on the keyboard so it becomes music rather than clapping.

Key word: Ostinato

A short pattern repeated over and over. If one of your bars keeps coming back, you have written an ostinato.

Need a hand? Steal a rhythm

Clap the rhythm of a sentence you say often, or of your own name repeated four times. Write down what you clapped. That is a composition and it is yours.

Go further

Write a second four bar rhythm that answers the first, so the two together sound like a question and a reply. Perform them one after the other.
