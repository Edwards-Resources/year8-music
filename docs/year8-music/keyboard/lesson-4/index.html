Black-Key Pentatonic Improvising

Lesson 4 of 25 · Pitch and the Keyboard

We are learning to improvise using the black keys, and to describe what we play.

Every black key fits.

You already know the black keys come in groups of two and three. Today your teacher holds down one low black key as a drone, and you improvise on the other black keys over the top. Try it before you think about it: every black key you play will sound like it belongs, because they are all part of the same five-note set. That set has a name, pentatonic, but you hear it works before you learn what to call it.

How to try it

  1. Teacher holds a drone on one low black key and keeps it going.
  2. Play any black keys you like, anywhere on the keyboard, in any order.
  3. Listen back: nothing you played clashed with the drone.
  4. Now do it in a call and response - teacher plays four black-key notes, you answer with four of your own.

Your task - Question and answer

In pairs, take turns. One of you plays a short black-key idea over the drone, four or five notes. Your partner answers with their own idea that fits the same mood. Swap after three turns each.

Key words

PentatonicImprovise

Pentatonic - a scale built from five notes rather than the usual seven. The black keys are a pentatonic scale on their own. Improvise - to make music up as you play it, rather than performing something already written down.

Need a hand? Word bank

Describe your idea using one of these words: swirling, dreamy, wandering, floating, spiky, playful, calm, mysterious. Finish the sentence: “My idea sounded ___ because I played ___.”

Go further

Swap roles: you hold the drone while your partner improvises, then compare how it feels to support someone else's idea instead of making your own.

Encore

Describe one black-key idea you played, using at least one word from the word bank.
