The C Major Scale, Right Hand

Lesson 12 of 25 · Scales and Hands Together

We are learning to perform a C major scale with correct fingering.

Video

The thumb tuck is the whole trick. Watch for it at the fourth note.

Eight notes, five fingers.

A scale runs C D E F G A B C, which is eight notes. You have five fingers. The thumb solves it: after the third note your thumb tucks under the hand and starts again, so you keep travelling without stopping.

Your task

Right hand, one octave, up and down. Start slowly enough that the thumb tuck is smooth rather than a jolt. Play it four times perfectly before you speed up at all.

Key words

ScaleFingering

Scale - a sequence of notes in order of pitch, the building blocks most melodies are made from. Fingering - which finger plays which note, chosen so the hand can keep moving.

Need a hand? Five notes first

Play C up to G and back, one finger per note, with no thumb tuck at all. Get that even and confident, then add the tuck as a separate job.

Go further

Play the scale over two octaves, then work out the fingering for G major and find where its one black key changes things.

Encore

Which finger plays F in the right hand C major scale?
