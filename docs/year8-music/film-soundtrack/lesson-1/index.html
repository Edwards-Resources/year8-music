Lesson 1: The Sound of Story

Lesson 1 of 25, in Sound and Story.

Learning intention

We are learning to identify how music and sound work together in film and games.

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

ListenStart here: one scene, watched twice

LearnWatch it twice.

Watch the first version and describe the music. How does it start, and how does it change? Did anything else about the scene stand out? Then watch the second version, with the music taken away, and see what the music was doing all along.

ListenThree scenes, three soundtracks

Table

Sound categoryWhat it meansExample
DiegeticA sound the characters in the story can hear tooA radio playing in the room, a band on stage
Non-diegeticMusic or sound added for the audience onlyThe orchestral score swelling under a chase
FoleyEveryday sound effects recreated and added afterwardsFootsteps, doors, wind, rustling paper

LearnSame toolkit, new job.

Term 3 you built a track from the elements of music. This term you use the same skills - rhythm, harmony, texture, dynamics - to serve a picture instead of a song. Nothing new to learn, a new job for what you already know.

Your task

Listen like a sound designer. Every scene is built from three kinds of sound, and today you sort them.

  • Write the three definitions below into your book: diegetic sound, non-diegetic sound and foley.
  • Rule up a table with three columns headed Diegetic, Non-diegetic and Foley.
  • Watch each scene. List everything you can hear in the right column - voices, music, footsteps, objects, crowds, anything.
  • Compare your table with the class. Where people disagree, test the sound with the question in the support box.

Key words

Diegetic sound
something the characters in the story could hear too.
Non-diegetic sound
music or sound added for the audience, that the characters cannot hear.
Foley
everyday sound effects recreated and layered onto a scene afterwards.

Need a hand?Ask one question

For any sound you’re not sure about, ask: could a character in the story actually hear this? Yes means diegetic. No means non-diegetic - unless it’s a sound effect like footsteps or a door, which is foley.

Go further

Find a leitmotif - a short musical idea tied to one character or place - that returns more than once in a film you know well. Explain what it represents and how you can tell.

Exit ticket

Name one sound from today that only the audience could hear, not the characters.
