Foley: Building Sound by Hand

Lesson 3 of 25 · Sound and Story

We are learning how foley artists create realistic sound effects.

Build your foley kit

  1. Choose two classroom objects and a sound you think they could make together.
  2. Test it near a phone or iPad microphone, close and quiet.
  3. Record it in GarageBand using the Audio Recorder.
  4. Listen back. Does it sound like the real thing, or like an object being hit?
  5. Try again with a different object, or closer to the mic, until it convinces you.
  6. Save the file with a clear name - what it's meant to be, not what it actually is.

Why it still matters

Even in a world of digital sound libraries, foley is usually recorded by a person performing to picture, because a real performance moves and breathes in time with the image in a way a library file never quite does.

Your task - Foley match

Close your eyes while a partner performs a foley sound. Guess the real object before they tell you.

Key word: Foley

Everyday sound effects - footsteps, doors, wind, crashes - created and added to a film or game to enhance realism. Named after Jack Foley, who pioneered the technique.

Need a hand? Start with one sound

Footsteps are the easiest place to start. Try shoes on different surfaces - carpet, tile, an empty box for a wooden floor - and walk in place with your hands, tapping the surface in rhythm.

Go further

Layer two or three foley sounds to build one complex effect - for example footsteps, a creaking door and distant wind together for a single haunted-house moment.

Encore

What everyday object surprised you as a sound effect today?
