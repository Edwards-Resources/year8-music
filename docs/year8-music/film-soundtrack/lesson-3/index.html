Foley: Building Sound by Hand

Lesson 3 of 25 · Sound and Story

We are learning how foley artists create realistic sound effects.

Foley artists at work

Why it still matters

Even in a world of digital sound libraries, foley is usually recorded by a person performing to picture, because a real performance moves and breathes in time with the image in a way a library file never quite does.

Your task - Act it out, sound it out

In your group, make up a short story you can act out in about 30 seconds. One person performs every sound effect live on the keyboard while the others act.

  • Plan a story with at least four moments that need a sound: footsteps, a door, a crash, the weather, anything.
  • Scroll through the keyboard's sounds and find one for each moment. Write down the sound's number so you can find it again.
  • Rehearse until every sound lands exactly when the action happens.
  • Perform for the class. The audience calls out which sounds worked best and why.

Key word: Foley

Everyday sound effects - footsteps, doors, wind, crashes - created and added to a film or game to enhance realism. Named after Jack Foley, who pioneered the technique.

Need a hand? Start with one sound

Start with footsteps or a door, the easiest sounds to time. Try the same sound in a low and a high register: lower usually sounds bigger and heavier, higher sounds smaller and lighter.

Go further

Layer two sounds at once for one moment, for example footsteps and wind together as a character walks through a storm.

Encore

Which keyboard sound was the most convincing sound effect today, and why?
