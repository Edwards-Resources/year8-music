Spotting: Where Does the Music Go?

Lesson 5 of 25 · Sound and Story

We are learning to plan where music and sound belong in a scene.

What is spotting?

Spotting is the planning stage of film scoring, where a director and composer watch a scene together and decide exactly where music starts, where it stops, and where silence does the work instead. Every soundtrack starts here, before a single note is written.

Your task - Spot the scene

Watch a muted clip your teacher plays. Mark the moments where you would add music, add an effect, or leave it silent.

Key word: Cue

A specific piece of music written for a particular moment in a film or game, decided during spotting and then composed to fit exactly.

Need a hand? Just mark three spots

Pick the beginning, the most dramatic moment, and the ending. Decide music, effect or silence for each of those three before trying the whole scene.

Go further

Justify every choice using the proper terms - cue, leitmotif, dynamics, texture - rather than just “it felt right here.”
