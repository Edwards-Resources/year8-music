Lesson 21: Deep Dive: A Feature Score

Lesson 21 of 25, in Reflect and Extend.

Learning intention

We are learning how music interacts with storytelling across a whole film.

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

Watch

A trailer built almost entirely on its score

LearnOne scene is a sample. A whole film is a system.

Everything you’ve studied this term - diegetic sound, leitmotif, spotting, dynamics - works together across an entire film, not just one excerpt. A feature score returns to its ideas, develops them, and times its silences across two hours, not two minutes.

LearnEvery deep dive opens with jazz.

Before you pick your film, the whole class hears the same noir cue: Henry Mancini’s Peter Gunn theme, a swung, syncopated bass riff under blaring horns. Jazz has scored film and television since sound arrived, from a muted trumpet under a detective’s footsteps to entire scores built on improvisation. Naming it here means you meet the mandatory jazz repertoire area no matter which film gets picked for the deep dive.

Key words

Synchronisation
The process of matching sound precisely with on-screen action - the same skill you practised in Lesson 15, at the scale of a whole film.
Swing
A jazz rhythmic feel where quaver pairs are played long-short instead of even, the feel under the Peter Gunn cue that opened this lesson.

Need a hand?Just three moments

Pick the opening, one dramatic turning point, and the ending. Note what the music is doing in each.

Go further

Analyse a stylistic influence in the score - jazz, classical, another genre - and explain how it shapes the film’s identity.

Exit ticket

Pick one moment where the music told you something the picture alone didn’t.
