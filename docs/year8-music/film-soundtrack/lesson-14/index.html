Composing the Underscore

Lesson 14 of 25 · Suspense and the Brief

We are learning to compose music that matches the mood of a scene.

Score your scene

  1. Review your spotting sheet from Lesson 12.
  2. For each cue, choose or write a loop or melody that matches that moment's mood.
  3. Fade music in and out rather than cutting it abruptly.
  4. Change the tempo, texture or dynamics at your scene's turning point.
  5. Save.

Reuse what you already know.

Drums, chords, bass, a riff, texture, effects - every skill from your Term 3 composition applies here. The difference is that the picture sets the timing now, not a repeating 8-bar loop.

Key word: Underscoring

Background music written to support the action, mood or dialogue of a scene. It's the music layer of your project, sitting under the dialogue and foley.

Need a hand? Use a pre-labelled loop bank

Choose from loops sorted by mood - tense, calm, triumphant - and place them under the matching moment in your scene. Choosing the right loop for the right moment is still a real composing decision.

Go further

Compose an original motif for your scene rather than using a pre-made loop, and orchestrate it across two or three instrument sounds.

Encore

Does your music enter and exit the scene, or does it just start and stop?
