Silent Films, Live Music

Lesson 6 of 25 · A History of Scoring

We are learning how music supported storytelling before recorded sound.

Video

Charlie Chaplin - Factory Scene - Modern Times (1936)

No dialogue, no effects, just the pianist.

Before synchronised sound, cinemas hired a pianist or organist to improvise music live under every screening. The same film could feel completely different from one cinema to the next, depending on who was playing.

Your task - Improvise a soundtrack

In pairs, try a simple improvised soundtrack over a muted clip, using GarageBand or a classroom instrument. React to what's happening on screen as it happens.

Key word: Score

The written or recorded music created specifically for a film or video game. In the silent era, the “score” was often whatever the pianist chose to play that night.

Need a hand? Follow a simple rule

Play softly and slowly for calm moments, faster and louder for excitement or danger. That's the whole job a silent-film pianist was doing, most of the time.

Go further

Research how different countries - Japan, the United States, Europe - approached silent film music differently, and why.
