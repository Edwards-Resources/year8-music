Strumming in 4/4

Lesson 12 of 25 · Chords and World Instruments

We are learning to strum a steady pattern in 4/4 time.

Video

Down, down, up, up, down. Watch the strumming arm, not the fretting hand.

The arm never stops.

Good strumming keeps your arm moving up and down on every beat, whether or not you actually hit the strings. That constant motion is what keeps a strumming pattern locked to the beat instead of drifting.

Build it in stages

  1. Practise the down-down-up-up-down pattern with no chord at all, just the motion.
  2. Add E minor and strum the pattern slowly, counting the beats out loud.
  3. Play along with a backing track at a slow tempo.
  4. Only once it's steady, try changing to A minor partway through the pattern.

Your task

Strum the down-down-up-up-down pattern on E minor until it's steady, then repeat on A minor. Finish by changing chords every four strums without breaking the pattern.

Key word: Strumming

Brushing across the strings with a pick or fingers to play a chord, rather than picking out individual notes.

Need a hand? Downs only, to start

Strum on the beat, down only, until that feels completely steady. Add the up-strokes in between once the downs are locked in.

Go further

Try a pattern that leaves out a strum on beat three, and work out how to keep your arm moving through the gap without losing time.

Encore

In the pattern down-down-up-up-down, which strums land on the beat?
