Timpani - the percussion family

Same job, different family.

Every orchestral instrument makes its sound one of four ways: a vibrating string, air blown across or into a reed, buzzing lips down a metal tube, or something struck, shaken or scraped. That method of sound production is what puts an instrument into its family.

Key words

String FamilyWoodwind FamilyBrass FamilyPercussion Family

String family - instruments that produce sound from vibrating strings, such as violin, cello and guitar. Woodwind family - instruments that produce sound by blowing air across a reed or opening, such as flute and clarinet. Brass family - instruments made of metal that use buzzing lips to create sound, such as trumpet and trombone. Percussion family - instruments that are struck, shaken or scraped to make sound, such as drums and xylophone.

Need a hand? How is the sound made?

Ignore what the instrument looks like and ask one question: how does it actually make its sound? Blown, bowed, buzzed or hit. That question sorts every instrument into its family.

Go further

The guitar is a string instrument, like the violin, but the two sound very different. Explain what about the way each one is played changes its tone colour.
