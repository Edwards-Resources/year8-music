Parts of the Guitar

Lesson 1 of 25 · Strings, Riffs and Tone Colour

We are learning to identify the parts of the guitar and hold it correctly.

Video

Six strings, six pegs. Watch how each one is turned to bring a string up or down to pitch.

Six strings, one map.

Every guitar has the same six parts doing the same six jobs, acoustic or electric. Learn the map once and it works on any guitar you ever pick up.

PartWhat it does
HeadstockHolds the tuning pegs at the top of the neck
Tuning PegsTurns to tighten or loosen a string and change its pitch
FretA metal strip across the neck that marks off each note
BodyThe hollow or solid block that the sound comes from
BridgeAnchors the strings at the bottom of the body

Your task

Label a diagram of the guitar with headstock, tuning pegs, frets, body and bridge. Then hold your guitar the way your teacher shows you and check with a partner: guitar resting on your leg, neck angled up, strumming arm relaxed.

Key words

HeadstockTuning PegsFret

Headstock - the top of the guitar that holds the tuning pegs. Tuning Pegs - turns to tighten or loosen a string and change its pitch. Fret - the metal strip across the neck of the guitar that divides notes.

Need a hand? Point before you name

Point to each part on your own guitar before you write its name down. If you can point to it, the label is easy; if you can't find it, ask before guessing.

Go further

Work out why nylon strings and steel strings need different amounts of turning to reach the same pitch, and check your idea with your teacher.

Encore

Point to your guitar's headstock, then its bridge.
