G and C, and Your First Progression

Lesson 13 of 25 · Chords and World Instruments

We are learning to play G and C chords and move between them fluently.

Video

G, C and D, and ten songs that use them. You only need G and C today.

A progression is a path.

A chord progression is a sequence of chords played one after another, and it's the structural backbone of most songs you know. G to C to D is one of the most common paths in guitar music, in any style.

Drilling a chord change

  1. Set up the first chord and check it rings clean before you move.
  2. Lift your fingers together and reset them on the second shape, as one movement.
  3. Repeat the same two-chord change for eight bars, counting the beats.
  4. Add a third chord only once the first change is smooth.

Your task

Learn G and C, then drill the change between them for eight bars with a partner giving feedback. If it's solid, try the progression G, C, D or Em, C, G, D.

Need a hand? Move one finger at a time

If G and C share no fingers in common, that's normal; look for the single easiest finger to place first each time and let the rest follow it into shape.

Go further

Add a sus2 or sus4 chord as a passing shape between two of your regular chords, and describe what it does to the progression.
