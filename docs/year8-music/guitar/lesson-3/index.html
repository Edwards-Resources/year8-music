Your First Riff

Lesson 3 of 25 · Strings, Riffs and Tone Colour

We are learning to perform a single-string riff in time with a steady beat.

Video

Smoke on the Water, on one string only. Watch the frets before you watch the fingers.

One string is enough.

A riff is a short pattern of notes, played over and over, that is instantly recognisable. Some of the most famous riffs in rock use only one string, because the pattern is what matters, not how many strings it crosses.

How to learn it

  1. Find the string and the first fret in the riff before you try to play it in time.
  2. Say the fret numbers out loud as you play them, slowly.
  3. Play it once at a speed with no mistakes at all.
  4. Only then add the backing beat, at 60 to 80 beats per minute.

Your task

Learn the riff on one string, following the video. Once you can play it three times without a mistake, play along with a steady drum loop at 60-80 bpm.

Key word: Riff

A short pattern of notes, often played on single strings, repeated so it becomes instantly recognisable.

Need a hand? One fret at a time

Play just the first two notes until they are solid, then add one more note at a time. A riff you have chunked into pairs is much easier than one you are trying to play whole.

Go further

Once the riff is solid at 60 bpm, take it up to 100 bpm without losing accuracy, and work out which note is hardest to keep clean at speed.
